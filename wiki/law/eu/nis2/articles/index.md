# Articles

Navigation index for Articles.

## Concepts

- [Article 1 NIS2 — Subject matter](article-1.md) — Establishes the subject matter and aims of Directive (EU) 2022/2555, including cybersecurity risk-management and reporting obligations, supervisory frameworks, and cooperation mechanisms.
- [Article 10 NIS2 — Computer security incident response teams (CSIRTs)](article-10.md) — Mandates the designation and operational operationalisation of national CSIRTs with continuous monitoring, alerting, vulnerability handling, and dynamic incident response capabilities.
- [Article 11 NIS2 — Co-ordinated vulnerability disclosure and a European vulnerability database](article-11.md) — Tasks national CSIRTs with coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD) facilitation and establishes the European Vulnerability Database (EUVD) administered by ENISA.
- [Article 12 NIS2 — Cooperation at national level](article-12.md) — Requires structured domestic coordination between competent authorities, national CSIRTs, law enforcement, data protection authorities, and CER critical entity supervisors.
- [Article 13 NIS2 — CSIRTs network](article-13.md) — Establishes the CSIRTs Network to foster operational cooperation, mutual assistance, incident information exchange, and joint incident exercises across Member States.
- [Article 14 NIS2 — Cooperation Group](article-14.md) — Establishes the NIS Cooperation Group to steer strategic policy guidance, exchange best practices, and evaluate cross-border sector risk assessments.
- [Article 15 NIS2 — European cyber crisis liaison organisation network (EU-CyCLONe)](article-15.md) — Creates EU-CyCLONe to support the coordinated management of large-scale cybersecurity incidents and crises at the operational and political levels.
- [Article 16 NIS2 — International cooperation](article-16.md) — Empowers the Union and Member States to conclude international cooperation agreements with third countries and international organisations on cybersecurity.
- [Article 17 NIS2 — Peer reviews](article-17.md) — Establishes a peer-review mechanism conducted by Member State cybersecurity experts and ENISA to evaluate the effectiveness of national cybersecurity policies and supervision.
- [Article 18 NIS2 — Assistance by ENISA](article-18.md) — Details ENISA's statutory support duties, including capacity building, tool development, incident reporting analysis, and technical guideline drafting.
- [Article 19 NIS2 — Coordinated security risk assessments of critical supply chains](article-19.md) — Empowers the Cooperation Group and Commission to execute Union-wide coordinated cybersecurity risk assessments of critical ICT services, systems, or supply chains.
- [Article 2: Scope & Size-Cap Rule](article-2.md) — Defines personal and material scope of NIS2 based on the size-cap rule (medium and large enterprises) and specific exceptions.
- [Article 20: Governance](article-20.md) — Mandates approval of cybersecurity measures by management bodies, personal liability, and mandatory training.
- [Article 21: Cybersecurity Risk-Management Measures](article-21.md) — Mandates ten baseline technical, operational, and organizational cybersecurity measures across an all-hazards approach.
- [Article 22 NIS2 — Union level coordinated vulnerability disclosure](article-22.md) — Establishes European-level coordination mechanisms for multi-party vulnerabilities facilitated by national CSIRTs and ENISA.
- [Article 23: Reporting Obligations](article-23.md) — Establishes the mandatory multi-stage incident reporting framework with strict 24h, 72h, and 1-month statutory clocks.
- [Article 24 NIS2 — Use of European cybersecurity certification schemes](article-24.md) — Authorises Member States and the Commission to mandate the use of certified ICT products, services, or processes under the Cybersecurity Act (Regulation (EU) 2019/881).
- [Article 25 NIS2 — Standardisation](article-25.md) — Encourages the development and adoption of open, harmonised European and international standards for cybersecurity risk management and incident reporting.
- [Article 26 NIS2 — Information-sharing arrangements](article-26.md) — Encourages essential and important entities to exchange cybersecurity notifications, threat intelligence, and defensive indicators within trustworthy sharing communities.
- [Article 27 NIS2 — Registry of entities](article-27.md) — Tasks ENISA with creating and maintaining a centralized registry of entities providing cross-border digital infrastructure and services.
- [Article 28 NIS2 — Database of domain name registration data](article-28.md) — Obligates TLD registries and domain registrars to maintain complete, accurate domain registration data (WHOIS) and enable lawful access to legitimate seekers.
- [Article 29 NIS2 — Jurisdiction and territoriality](article-29.md) — Establishes that entities fall under the jurisdiction of the Member State where they are established, subject to specific cross-border rules for digital infrastructure.
- [Article 3: Essential and Important Entities Classification](article-3.md) — Establishes statutory criteria distinguishing essential entities from important entities and supervisory implications.
- [Article 30 NIS2 — National list of essential and important entities](article-30.md) — Requires Member States to establish and submit to the Commission a complete registry list of identified essential and important entities by 17 April 2025.
- [Article 31 NIS2 — General aspects of supervision and enforcement](article-31.md) — Directs competent authorities to exercise proportionate, dissuasive supervisory powers and enforce risk-management duties.
- [Article 32 NIS2 — Supervisory and enforcement measures in relation to essential entities](article-32.md) — Equips authorities with ex-ante supervisory powers over essential entities, including regular on-site audits, off-site targeted inspections, and security scans.
- [Article 33 NIS2 — Supervisory and enforcement measures in relation to important entities](article-33.md) — Establishes ex-post supervisory mechanisms for important entities, triggered when evidence or indication of non-compliance is presented.
- [Article 34: Administrative Fines and Penalties](article-34.md) — Establishes statutory administrative fine maximums up to €10M or 2% turnover for essential entities, and €7M or 1.4% turnover for important entities.
- [Article 35 NIS2 — Infringements entailing a personal data breach](article-35.md) — Requires competent authorities to notify supervisory data protection authorities under GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) without undue delay upon detecting data breaches.
- [Article 36 NIS2 — Penalties](article-36.md) — Mandates Member States to establish national statutory rules on penalties applicable to infringements of national transposing legislation.
- [Article 37 NIS2 — Mutual assistance](article-37.md) — Obligates competent authorities across Member States to provide mutual supervisory and enforcement assistance for cross-border entities.
- [Article 38 NIS2 — Exercise of the delegation](article-38.md) — Governs the conditions under which the Commission may adopt delegated acts amending non-essential elements of the Directive.
- [Article 39 NIS2 — Committee procedure](article-39.md) — Establishes the comitology committee assisting the Commission in adopting implementing acts under the Directive.
- [Article 4 NIS2 — Sector-specific Union legal acts](article-4.md) — Establishes the lex specialis principle, clarifying that sector-specific Union acts (e.g. DORA for financial services) take precedence where their requirements are at least equivalent in effect.
- [Article 40 NIS2 — Review](article-40.md) — Requires the European Commission to periodically review the functioning of the Directive and submit an evaluation report to the European Parliament by 17 October 2027.
- [Article 41 NIS2 — Transposition](article-41.md) — Sets the statutory deadline of 17 October 2024 for Member States to adopt and publish national transposing laws, applying them from 18 October 2024.
- [Article 42 NIS2 — Amendment of Directive (EU) 2018/1972](article-42.md) — Amends the European Electronic Communications Code to align telecom cybersecurity supervision with NIS2.
- [Article 43 NIS2 — Amendment of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014](article-43.md) — Amends the eIDAS Regulation to harmonize security requirements and supervision for trust service providers.
- [Article 44 NIS2 — Repeal](article-44.md) — Formally repeals the original NIS Directive (Directive (EU) 2016/1148) with effect from 18 October 2024.
- [Article 45 NIS2 — Entry into force](article-45.md) — Provides that the Directive enters into force on the twentieth day following its publication in the Official Journal (16 January 2023).
- [Article 46 NIS2 — Addressees](article-46.md) — Designates the Member States as the addressees of the Directive.
- [Article 5 NIS2 — Minimum harmonisation](article-5.md) — Affirms that Directive (EU) 2022/2555 is a minimum harmonisation directive, permitting Member States to adopt or maintain higher levels of cybersecurity protection.
- [Article 6 NIS2 — Definitions](article-6.md) — Defines the core statutory terms of the Directive, including network and information system, incident, near miss, cybersecurity risk, cyber threat, vulnerability, and representative.
- [Article 7 NIS2 — National cybersecurity strategy](article-7.md) — Mandates each Member State to adopt a comprehensive national cybersecurity strategy setting strategic objectives, policy governance, governance structures, and resource commitments.
- [Article 8 NIS2 — Competent authorities and single points of contact](article-8.md) — Requires Member States to designate one or more competent authorities responsible for cybersecurity and a Single Point of Contact (SPOC) to liaise across borders.
- [Article 9 NIS2 — National cyber crisis management frameworks](article-9.md) — Directs Member States to designate national competent authorities for cyber crisis management and integrate into the EU-CyCLONe operational framework.
