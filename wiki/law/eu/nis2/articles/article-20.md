# Summary

**Article 20 (Governance)** of Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (NIS2)[^directive-eu-2022-2555] represents a historic shift in European cybersecurity law by making cybersecurity governance an explicit statutory duty of corporate boards and management bodies.

Under Article 20, management bodies of essential and important entities are legally required to approve cybersecurity risk-management measures, oversee their implementation, and may be held personally liable for breaches of their obligations.

# Core Statutory Obligations

### 1. Board Approval & Oversight (Article 20(1))
- **Mandatory Approval**: The management bodies of essential and important entities must formally approve the cybersecurity risk-management measures taken by those entities under Article 21.
- **Active Oversight**: Management bodies must oversee the implementation of those measures and can be held liable for non-compliance by the entity.

### 2. Mandatory Management Training (Article 20(2))
- **Executive Competence**: Members of the management bodies must follow regular cybersecurity training in order to gain sufficient knowledge and skills to identify risks, assess cybersecurity risk-management practices, and evaluate their impact on the services provided by the entity.
- **Workforce Training**: Regulated entities must encourage management bodies to offer similar cybersecurity training to all employees on a regular basis.

# Enforcement & Personal Liability Mechanisms
Under Article 32 (Supervisory and enforcement measures for essential entities), national competent authorities possess the legal power to:
- Suspend certification or authorization of services.
- Impose temporary bans against any person discharging managerial responsibilities at chief executive officer (CEO) or legal representative level from exercising managerial functions.
